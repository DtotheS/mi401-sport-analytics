Course overview

Sport analytics brings together data, models, and analytical tools to study questions across the sports domain. This course introduces how sports data are collected, prepared, analyzed, visualized, and communicated. Applications include team performance, player evaluation, sport media, sport business, and fan engagement, with examples from multiple sports.

Models will be introduced through their purpose, basic logic, outputs, and limitations rather than advanced mathematics or deep theoretical treatment. Class time will emphasize guided, hands-on use of accessible tools and interpretation of results. These skills are useful for sport analytics and transferable to broader careers in data science and analytics. No prior programming experience or sport analytics background is assumed, and no programming is required.

Prerequisites and recommended background

MI 101 and completion of the Tier I writing requirement. MI 304 is recommended or may be taken concurrently.

Learning objectives

  1. Identify useful analytical questions across sport performance, player evaluation, media, business, and fan engagement.
  2. Locate sport datasets and evaluate their source, structure, quality, privacy considerations, limitations, and appropriate use.
  3. Prepare small and medium-sized datasets for analysis using Excel.
  4. Use selected supervised and unsupervised machine-learning models and interpret their outputs, basic validation results, uncertainty, and limitations.
  5. Conduct introductory text analysis and structured video coding, and explain what each method can and cannot show.
  6. Create effective visualizations, dashboards, and data stories for nontechnical audiences.
  7. Communicate analytical findings clearly and revise the work into a portfolio-ready product relevant to sport analytics or broader analytics careers.

Course content and learning approach

The course follows an applied workflow rather than a fixed weekly sequence:

  1. Data discovery and collection. A brief introduction to finding sport datasets and to common collection methods, including official league/team sources, Kaggle, APIs, surveys, sensors, tracking systems, social media, and video.
  2. Data preparation. Cleaning, recoding, joining, documenting, and checking data in Excel.
  3. Machine learning. An applied introduction to selected supervised models for classification or prediction and unsupervised models for clustering or pattern discovery, including basic model comparison, validation, and interpretation in WEKA. Emphasis is on what models do and how to read their outputs rather than derivations or deep theory.
  4. Text and video analysis. Exploratory text analysis in Voyant and structured event coding in a browser-based video tool, with a short computer-vision demonstration when useful.
  5. Visualization and communication. Charts, dashboards, model summaries, analytical findings, and data stories in Tableau.
  6. Critical understanding of analytics. Data quality, ethics, privacy, labor, fairness, accessibility, uncertainty, and the limits of analytical models.

Examples, cases, and datasets will draw from different sports, countries, competition levels, and industry settings. Students do not need to be sport experts or sport fans.

Materials and technology

There is no required textbook. Short readings, cases, videos, datasets, and tutorials will be provided through D2L at no additional cost.

Setup and access instructions will be provided during the course.
  3. Accounts and accessibility. Some third-party tools may require a free account. If an account, platform, accessibility, or privacy requirement creates a barrier, an equivalent alternative will be provided.

Course policies and expectations

Attendance and participation

Regular attendance matters because class sessions include hands-on labs, analytical activities, and discussion. Attendance is not graded separately. In-class activity credit is based on submitted work, while participation credit reflects preparation and constructive engagement.

If you must miss class, notify the instructor as soon as practical and obtain notes or materials. An unexcused absence receives a zero for activities completed during that class. For absences protected by university policy or otherwise approved by the instructor, a reasonable alternative will be provided when feasible.

Communication

D2L is the official source for announcements, materials, assignment instructions, and due dates. Students should enable notifications and check the course site regularly. The instructor normally responds to email within two business days; messages sent near a deadline may not receive an immediate response.

Generative AI and analytical assistance

Unless an assignment states otherwise, generative AI may be used to brainstorm questions, clarify concepts, troubleshoot software, and improve wording. It may not replace the student’s analysis, fabricate data or sources, or generate work that the student cannot explain. Students remain responsible for checking accuracy, protecting data, and meeting assignment requirements.

When generative AI materially contributes to submitted work, include a brief disclosure naming the tool, describing how it was used, and identifying what the student verified or changed. Assignment-specific directions may permit, limit, or prohibit AI use.

Academic integrity and collaboration

Students are expected to submit original work and clearly credit data sources, ideas, quotations, code, templates, outside help, and permitted AI assistance. Collaboration is encouraged when an activity allows it, but each student must complete individually assigned work and be able to explain submitted analyses. Fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, unauthorized collaboration, and presenting another person’s work as one’s own are academic misconduct under MSU policy.

Students who need to miss class or other coursework for a religious observance must notify the instructor at least two weeks before the observance day. Reasonable arrangements will be made without academic penalty.

Reasonable arrangements will be made for verified grief absences. Students affected by illness, emergency, or an approved university activity should contact the instructor as soon as practicable.
